# Interview 01b: travel DMC, follow-up call

Date: 2026-11-15. Recorded, transcript available. Second call with the same subject.
Target: the Maldives annual settlement he volunteered at the end of call one.

## The Maldives settlement, fully mapped

This is the one real finding across both calls, and it is cleaner than expected.

**How it works:**

- Commission from Maldives resorts is settled **once a year, in February.**
- Settlement happens **in person**. He travels to Maldives for an exhibition, then the
  resorts come to his office and **hand over the commission in cash, in USD.**
- What he receives is **a single total.** "Total booking, total commission." No
  per-booking breakdown, no statement, no document.
- Volume: **500+ bookings a year**, across **300+ resorts.**
- Commission rate: **5 to 10%**, differs by resort. Some resorts pay it directly, for
  others he has to retain a sub-commission.
- **Rates change every month.**
- His own entitlement record lives **in the contract**, and the booking data sits in
  email. He acknowledged it could be pulled into Excel or the CRM. It is not.
- His own numbers do not match anyway, because bookings mutate late: cancellations,
  guests extending stays, guests buying extras. "Everything will be changed around last
  late."

**Whether he reconciles it:**

> "Do you compare the settlement against your list of bookings?"
> **"No, we don't."**
>
> "You just trust them fully."
> **"Yes, yes, yes."**
>
> "So we don't need to track."

Asked how he would detect ten missing bookings out of five hundred, he deflected twice,
then said the resorts explain each line verbally at the annual meeting and that he could
produce proof if something were missing.

> "Have you ever queried a settlement amount?"
> **"No, no, no. Everything is perfect."**

**The structural read:** 500 bookings a year, across 300+ counterparties, at rates
between 5 and 10% that change monthly, against bookings that change shape after sale,
settled once annually as an undocumented cash lump sum in a foreign currency, **never
reconciled and never once queried in nine years.**

He is confident because it has never been shown to be wrong. It could never have been
shown to be wrong, because no one has ever checked. Those are different things.

**Arithmetic to get from him next time.** He would not give a typical commission per
booking ("it depends"). The contract has the rates and the email has the bookings, so he
can compute it himself: 500 bookings times average package value times 5 to 10%. Whatever
that number is, it arrives in cash once a year with no line items. A 3% shortfall would
be invisible and would be real money. **Do not estimate this for him. Get him to do the
multiplication out loud.**

## The automation objection got stronger

Call one had him criticizing MakeMyTrip. Call two gave it evidence and a mechanism:

- **Clients actively defect to him from MakeMyTrip and ClearTrip** over cancellation
  handling. It is an acquisition channel, not a hypothesis.
- His theory of why: "we are humans, we know the feelings of the clients, we will be
  getting the documents within 10 minutes in WhatsApp or mail, and then we will be
  proceeding. In software it's totally different, it will take some time."
- And a sharp observation about where the industry has pointed its automation: everyone
  concentrates on lead generation, "most of the companies are lagging in cancellation."

In his segment, automation is a churn source rather than a selling point. Any pitch has
to survive this, and it is better heard here than from Amit.

**The one question that did not get asked and should have:** whether something that did
all the paperwork and chasing, while he still made every decision, would feel different
to him. That separates judgment from clerical work, and it is the question his whole
objection turns on. Get it next time.

## The Rs 64,000 loss reclassified

It was not a booking error. A **new salesperson sent the client a photo of the wrong
bus.** The actual booking matched the contract and the itinerary: a 17-seater semi-sleeper.
The client had been shown a double-decker and expected that.

So it is an expectation-setting failure by an undertrained new hire, not a data or
process error. Cost Rs 64,000, absorbed because the client was his friend.

Then, in the same answer, he said: "till now there is no loss for us. Everything is
perfect."

## The pattern across both calls, and this is the real takeaway

**He reports perfection while describing losses.** Twice:

1. Call one: "there is no outstanding commission," then describes an unverifiable
   500-booking annual settlement.
2. Call two: "till now there is no loss for us, everything is perfect," one minute after
   describing a Rs 64,000 loss from the previous week.

This is not him being evasive. He is a competent operator with nine years of a working
business, and "everything is systemized" is how he understands himself, accurately in
most respects. The losses genuinely do not register as losses.

**That is Hemant's FDE thesis demonstrated empirically, twice, on tape.** The customer
cannot tell you their problem. Ask directly and they will tell you they do not have one.
Any product that depends on the customer recognizing and reporting the problem will never
be bought.

## The other things worth recording

- **2 AM escalations are rare**, six months to a year apart. There is a WhatsApp group per
  client containing all 15 teammates. He took the Thursday call only because the client
  was his friend. **Language is not an issue: "English is enough for every country."**
  Kill the multilingual angle.
- **His self-diagnosed constraint is demand, not operations.** He wants an influencer,
  storytelling and branding. Reference bookings from BNI convert at 80%; Instagram leads
  are much weaker, and he personally grades them hot/warm/cold in the CRM each week.
- **His stated cap on growth is geopolitics.** Asked what is slowing the company down:
  "these guys from Israel and Iran, they guys are fighting, right? That's the major
  issue." He noted larger agencies cutting sales headcount for the same reason.

## Verdict on this subject

**Real pain exists. He is not the customer.**

The Maldives settlement is a genuine, structurally guaranteed exposure. But he does not
experience it as a problem, has never tried to measure it, operates at a scale where he
would not fund a fix, and holds a reasoned belief that automation in his industry damages
the customer experience. Selling him finance automation means first convincing him he has
a problem, which is the hardest sale there is.

**Keep for three things:** the fully mapped unverified-settlement structure, the
automation objection with its evidence, and the twice-recorded contradiction between
reported perfection and described loss.

## Why limestone is now the right next call

Both businesses have the same structure, a counterparty that computes the number. The
difference is how the loss arrives:

- **Travel: the resort quietly hands over a lump sum.** The loss is silent, unmeasurable,
  and the owner reports zero problems.
- **Limestone (hypothesis): the cement plant actively deducts** and pays short against a
  known invoice. The loss is visible and the owner can feel it.

If that holds, the finding is sharper than either business alone: **the businesses with
silent losses need the product most and know it least.** Which is precisely why the
problem has to be detected from outside rather than reported from inside, and precisely
why Zamp needs FDEs today.
